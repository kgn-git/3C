    3C — the maturity model as a portfolio plan¶

    L1 → L4 isn't a checklist to complete. It's a set of places a team can choose to be, each answering a real question and each carrying its own, clearly-stated level of commitment. Adopt only the level whose risk you actually need to retire — and stay there as long as it serves you. Most teams find L1 is plenty for a long time.

    Locate your team¶

    Three yes/no questions. No JavaScript — expand the answer that matches.

    1. Do two engineers using AI on the same repo produce structurally consistent code without a reviewer forcing it?

    No → you are at L0. What you need next: L1 (Standardise) — shared conventions in every AI's context. Start at what L1 ships today.

    Yes → you are at least L1. Continue to question 2.

    2. Are your quality gates (lint, types, tests, secrets) run automatically before code review, every time?

    No → you are at L1. What you need next: L2 (Automate) — the gates stop being aspirational.

    Yes → you are at least L2. Continue to question 3.

    3. Can you show, with data, how AI is affecting your team's quality and velocity — and where the ROI is?

    No → you are at L2. What you need next: L3 (Observe) — telemetry becomes dashboards: quality trends, velocity, process adherence, ROI.

    Yes → you are at L3+. L3 (Observe) and L4 (Optimise) are on the roadmap — under active study, not yet available. What they aim at: dashboards and ROI attribution (L3), then governance that adapts to change complexity (L4).

    The four levels¶

    L1 — Standardise¶

    Shipped — May 2026

    • The question it answers: Is our team's AI converging or diverging?
    • Business risk addressed: architectural drift — every AI session locally optimal, globally incoherent.
    • Commitment level: Reversible — one PR to remove.
    • Calendar anchor: internal — adopt in one week (a focused adoption sprint), on your team's own calendar.

    L2 — Automate¶

    Shipped

    • The question it answers: Is our quality bar automatic or aspirational?
    • Business risk addressed: review-burden inflation — humans hand-checking what a gate should catch.
    • Commitment level: Rebuildable — telemetry can be disabled.
    • Calendar anchor: internal — paced to your team's quality-debt curve. A focused half-week adoption sprint from install to measurable deltas.

    L2 adds 21 features layered on top of L1's 9: extended rule families (CWE Top 25, microservices, modular-monolith), automated quality gates (Post-Edit Validation, Coverage Gate, Security Scan Gate, Architecture Boundary Enforcer, Hook Telemetry), workflow expansions (Deployment Skill, Bug Report, Onboarding, Multi-PM MCP, init --brand white-label scaffolder, extended test frameworks), and DX additions (Interactive Onboarding Guide, Coaching Mode, Standards Rationale Database, extended starter subagents architect + security-reviewer, Personal Local Insights Dashboard). The L2 surface then grew a multi-agent crew — eight specialist review agents (architect, code-reviewer, security-reviewer, test-author, product-owner, ux-expert, qa-reviewer, journey-architect) coordinated by three orchestrator skills (/3c-review-board for a reconciled multi-lens verdict, /3c-project-manager for the per-issue delivery pipeline, /3c-programme-manager for dependency-ordered delivery across a workspace's projects). It automates the review-and-delivery process a senior engineer runs by hand, and its per-change verdicts are the raw material the L3 layer rolls up. The full inventory and adoption path: what L2 ships today.

    L3 — Observe¶

    Roadmap — not yet available. Under active study; the specification is being refined from adopter feedback. No release date.

    • The question it aims to answer: Can we see, with data, how AI is affecting our quality, velocity, and ROI?
    • Business risk it targets: decision-blindness — scaling AI-assisted change with no measured view of its quality and velocity impact.
    • Direction: telemetry captured at L2 becomes team-observability dashboards — quality trends, velocity/DORA, ROI, process adherence, a cross-project view.
    • Want to shape it? L3 is where adopter input matters most — tell us what you'd need to see.

    L4 — Optimise¶

    Roadmap — not yet available. The furthest-out level; under study, its shape will be refined from adopter feedback as L3 lands. No release date.

    • The question it aims to answer: Is our governance adapting to our complexity?
    • Business risk it targets: governance ossification — one-size rules taxing simple changes, under-guarding complex ones.
    • Direction: a closed loop where telemetry feeds retrospectives that evolve standards, with complexity-adapted governance and policy-as-code.
    • Want to shape it? Start a discussion — the earlier the input, the more it counts.

    What we're learning

    Teams tell us the hardest part is deciding not to climb a level they don't need yet. If you're weighing that, we're happy to think it through with you: start a discussion.
